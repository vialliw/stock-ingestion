# Independent GLD/GDX Boom-Bust Review

**Purpose:** independent second pass on the 2025-26 gold/miner move, the
March-2026 volume profile and fund-flow pivot, and what the drawdown can (and
cannot) say about semiconductor correction size.

**Local market data:** `daily_close` through 2026-11-16. Profiles below use
2025-10-01 through 2026-03-31. Prices are ETF OHLC, not spot gold or NAV.
The flow figures are from issuer filings or named third-party flow trackers.

## Bottom line

- There was not one shared March top. GLD's primary blow-off was January 29;
  GDX's close high was February 27, with a higher intraday print on March 2.
  March was principally the liquidation leg, not the start of the GLD top.
- The March selloff was sharp but not the final low for either product.
  GDX's July 17 low was about 40% below its February close high; GLD continued
  to make lower lows into November, about 29% below its January close high.
- Fund flows are smaller than the AUM change and should not be conflated with
  it. GLD's SEC filing shows Q1 net redemptions of $3.34B, while vendor
  estimates put March alone at $7.9B-$8.5B. GDX's SEC-derived monthly data show
  a $661M March net outflow.
- A GDX-sized move applied to SMH's June 22 close peak gives about $403. The
  GLD-sized move gives about $475. SMH had already reached $445.59 by November
  5, so the gold-miner analogue would require another roughly 9.6% decline from
  that low; it is a scenario, not a timing or price forecast.

## 1. Event stages

The stages below deliberately separate the January volume climax, the
late-February/early-March marginal high, and the March break.

| Stage | GLD | GDX | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| October base / shakeout | Oct 1-31: $356.03 to $368.12, +3.4%; daily volume averaged 24.0M | Oct 1-31: $76.57 to $71.54, -6.6%; average 33.3M | GDX was already volatile and lagged while GLD held its base. |
| Markup | Nov 3-Jan 15: $368.78 to $423.33, +14.8%; average 11.4M | Nov 3-Jan 15: $70.79 to $97.11, +37.2%; average 22.4M | Miners showed much greater beta; GLD's average volume was less than half its October level. |
| Blow-off / first crack | Jan 29: $509.70 intraday high, $495.90 close; Jan 16-Feb 2 average volume 38.8M | Jan 29-Feb 2 window high $113.50; Jan 16-Feb 2 average volume 40.4M; window close-to-close -3.1% | The volume climax and first reversal preceded GDX's final high. |
| Distribution / marginal second high | Feb 3-Mar 6: $454.29 to $473.51 close, +4.2%; high $492.15 | Feb 3-Mar 6: $98.22 to $101.38 close, +3.2%; close high $115.84 Feb 27; intraday high $117.18 Mar 2 | GDX made the clearer marginal high. Neither fund closed at its January/February climax high. |
| March break | Mar 9-31: close $472.53 to $430.29, -8.9%; intramonth low $399.20 Mar 30 | Mar 9-31: close $102.44 to $91.77, -10.4%; intramonth low $78.74 Mar 20 | The close-to-close move understates the intramonth flush; both rebounded before month-end. |
| Post-break decline / low | Apr-Jul close $437.82 to $371.54; later low $351.96 Nov 16, -29.0% from Jan 29 close high | Apr-Jul close $96.01 to $74.10; low $69.74 Jul 17, -39.8% from Feb 27 close high | GDX completed a sharp cyclical drawdown; GLD's decline was slower and remained open in this dataset. |
| Reflex rally / retest | Aug 25 close high $428.07, then fell to $352.93 by Nov 16 | Aug 25 close high $105.52, then fell to $78.95 by Nov 16 | A large rebound did not establish a durable low. |

**Read:** the useful analogue is not “March outflow equals immediate bottom.”
GLD and GDX both had rebound windows after March, and the final low arrived
months later. Their different low dates also argue against using one fixed
gold-to-semi timing rule.

## 2. March 31 volume profiles

Method: 60 equal-price bins over each fund's daily low-to-high range from
2025-10-01 through 2026-03-31. Each day's reported volume is allocated across
the bins in proportion to the overlap of the day's high-low interval. The
70% value area is expanded contiguously from the point-of-control bin toward
the larger adjacent-volume bin.

| ETF | Profile range | POC | 70% value area | Mar 31 close |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GLD | $351.40-$509.70 | ~$387.02 | $354.04-$449.02 | $430.29 |
| GDX | $67.64-$117.18 | ~$77.13 | $67.64-$97.36 | $91.77 |
| SMH | $314.07-$427.94 | ~$337.80 | $325.46-$395.68 | $383.40 |

**Interpretation:** the March 31 GLD close remained inside its modeled value
area, in its upper half; GDX also remained inside its modeled value area.
SMH's March close was near the upper end of its own October-to-March value
area. These are historical acceptance zones, not guaranteed support or
resistance.

The POCs are similar to the prior 20-bin calculation, but value-area edges
move materially with bin count and construction method. Daily OHLCV cannot
reveal where trades occurred within each bar; this is a profile
approximation, not tick-level volume-at-price.

## 3. AUM and flows at the pivot

### GLD: separate official quarter totals from March-only estimates

The SPDR Gold Trust's March 31, 2026 Form 10-Q reports:

| Measure | Official filing value |
|---|---:|
| Net assets, Dec 31 2025 | $148.23B |
| Net assets, Mar 31 2026 | $155.11B |
| Q1 creations | $17.67B |
| Q1 redemptions | $21.01B |
| Q1 net flow | **-$3.34B** |
| Shares, Dec 31 -> Mar 31 | 374.2M -> 366.4M |
| Six-month net flow, Oct 1 2025-Mar 31 2026 | **+$4.86B** |

This is the cleanest flow anchor. Q1 is the calendar quarter January-March;
the filing does not break these totals out by month. Despite Q1 net
redemptions, reported net assets rose by $6.88B from December 31 to March 31.
That is why an AUM change cannot be treated as a flow estimate.

For March alone, Morningstar reports a $7.9B GLD outflow while ETF.com figures
repeated by Benzinga put it at about $8.5B. Treat **$7.9B-$8.5B** as a
vendor-dependent estimate, not an issuer-reported exact number. At roughly
$183B-$185B of late-February AUM, it is around **4.3%-4.6% of AUM**. Against
the roughly $28B-$30B estimated late-February-to-March-end AUM decline, the
reported flow accounts for about 27%-30%; most of the AUM decrease is
valuation loss.

There is a reconciliation warning: Nasdaq/ETFChannel reported 380.0M shares
on Feb 25, while another market-data page reports 385.2M on Feb 27; the
issuer's exact March 31 count is 366.4M. These snapshots do not cleanly
reconcile to the $7.9B-$8.5B vendor estimate. The SEC's quarter-level net
flow, and not a single third-party March figure, is the most defensible anchor.

### GDX: smaller dollars, material relative drawdown

SEC-filing-derived monthly creation/redemption data report **$683M created
versus $1.3B redeemed in March**, or **-$661M net**. With reported March 31
net assets of about $28.2B, that is roughly **2.3% of AUM**. February had
about +$1.0B net inflow; October-February net flows were approximately
+$2.31B. The March redemption was a clear reversal, but much smaller in
dollars and as a fraction of AUM than GLD's vendor-estimated flow.

### What the AUM asymmetry says

GDX's eventual peak-to-trough price decline was about 10 percentage points
larger than GLD's, despite smaller reported March ETF outflows. That is
consistent with operating leverage and equity-market risk in miners; ETF
redemptions alone do not explain the drawdown. For semiconductor comparisons,
GDX is therefore a more relevant *equity-beta severity case* than GLD, but
neither is a direct sector analogue.

## 4. Semiconductor comparison: severity, not timing

Local SMH data show a June 22, 2026 close peak of $668.91 and a November 5
low of $445.59, a **33.4%** peak-to-low decline. The November 16 close was
$461.90, or **30.9%** below the peak.

| Historical GLD/GDX drawdown | Applied to SMH $668.91 peak | Comparison with SMH Nov 5 low |
|---|---:|---|
| GLD: 29.0% peak-close to Nov 16 low | ~$474.80 | Already exceeded by the $445.59 low |
| GDX: 39.8% peak-close to Jul 17 low | ~$402.70 | Would imply another ~9.6% below $445.59 |

An ex-post profile of SMH's later run-up (Oct 1, 2025-Jun 22, 2026) gives a
60-bin POC of about $394.57; its densest bins cluster around $386-$409. This
places a GDX-sized price scenario near a historical volume concentration.
**Important:** that profile uses April-June 2026 data and was not knowable
from the March 31 snapshot. It must not be presented as a March-origin
prediction. On the strictly as-of-March profile, SMH's POC was ~$337.80.

Flow evidence also weakens a simple “outflows confirm capitulation” call.
ETF.com reports about $4.47B of SMH inflows in July even as the sector sold
off; Morningstar's separate July estimate is about $3.0B. ETF Channel then
estimated a roughly $678M SMH outflow for the week around Oct 15, but ETF.com
reported about $2.5B of SMH inflows in the week ending Oct 23. Provider
windows differ, but the sequence shows dip-buying and liquidation alternating,
not a clean one-way redemption signal.

**Scenario conclusion:** a 30%-40% semiconductor correction from the June
peak brackets roughly $468-$402. A GDX-severity central analogue is near
$403, with the later SMH volume concentration supporting that area
hindsight-wise. The gold evidence does not establish that $403 must trade,
that a low is due on the GDX timetable, or that ETF outflows alone can
estimate the semiconductor drawdown.
